## Statement

Bring the decision you are about to make and the reasoning behind it, not the question.

## In plain terms

A **manager of one** brings a **decision with reasoning** rather than a question. Not "what should
we do about the migration?" but "I am planning to do X because Y — tell me if that is wrong." The
second is easier to answer, faster to correct, and it demonstrates judgment that the first
conceals.

## Decides

How to bring a problem to someone senior.

## Why it holds

A question transfers the work. "What should we do?" requires the other person to load the context,
consider the options and produce an answer — most of which you have already done, and they have less
information than you.

A proposal inverts that. They read your reasoning, check it against what they know that you do not,
and either confirm or correct — which takes a fraction of the time and uses their comparative
advantage rather than duplicating yours.

It also produces better corrections. A stated reasoning chain can be corrected at the specific step
that is wrong; a question produces an answer with no visible reasoning, which teaches you nothing
about how they got there.

The demonstration effect is real and worth naming. Bringing proposals is how you become someone who
gets given scope, because it is direct evidence of judgment — and bringing questions repeatedly is
how you stay at the level you are at, regardless of how good the questions are.

The important caveat is that this is not about never asking. A genuine question — one where you
lack information, not judgment — is exactly right, and dressing it up as a proposal to appear
decisive wastes everyone's time.

## Example

An engineer discovers that a planned migration will conflict with a partner integration.

The question version: "The migration conflicts with the Acme integration — what do you think we
should do?" The manager now has to learn the conflict, understand both timelines, consider the
options and decide, from a worse position than the person asking.

The proposal version: "The migration conflicts with the Acme integration in week three. I am
planning to sequence the migration first and delay Acme by two weeks, because Acme has slack in
their timeline and our migration is blocking two other teams. The risk is that Acme's contract has a
date I have not seen. Do you know of one?"

That is answerable in thirty seconds. The manager checks it against what they know — and here they
do know something, which is that Acme's date is contractual — so the correction is precise: "the
Acme date is fixed, sequence the other way."

The engineer learns the specific fact they were missing rather than receiving an answer with no
reasoning. And the manager spent thirty seconds rather than an hour.

The second version also revealed the gap deliberately. Naming the unknown — "the risk is a contract
date I have not seen" — is what made the correction fast.

## Limits

It requires enough context to form a view, and there are situations where you genuinely do not have
one. Forcing a proposal when you have no basis for it produces a confident wrong plan, which is
worse than a question.

It also assumes a relationship where being corrected is fine. Where proposals are treated as
commitments, or where being wrong is expensive, people revert to questions for good reasons — and
that is a signal about the environment rather than about the person.

And it can shade into not asking for help. Someone genuinely stuck who keeps bringing proposals to
avoid appearing uncertain is doing the wrong thing well, and the honest version includes "I do not
know" as an available answer.

## Source

The phrase comes from *Rework*, describing people who can set their own direction without
supervision, and it has been widely adopted in engineering to describe the specific habit of
bringing decisions rather than questions.

Fournier's treatment in *The Manager's Path* is the more useful one for the mechanics, particularly
on what a manager actually needs — early warning, compressed context, and a recommendation they can
back in ten seconds.
